# How to Write an ATS-Friendly Resume in 2026

Applicant Tracking Systems (ATSs) reject up to 75% of resumes before a human ever sees them. Whether you're applying to a Tel Aviv startup or a global enterprise with local offices, understanding how ATSs work will dramatically increase your chances.

## What ATSs Actually Do

An ATS parses your resume into structured data — name, contact, work history, education, skills — and then scores it against the job description. The scoring is primarily keyword-based: does your resume contain the words the hiring manager put in the description?

Common ATS platforms used in Israel: Greenhouse, Lever, Workable, Comeet, and SmartRecruiters.

## The Core Rules

### 1. Use a clean, single-column layout

ATSs struggle with multi-column layouts, tables, headers/footers, and text boxes. Use a single flowing column. Resumaker's templates are all ATS-tested.

### 2. Match the job description literally

If the job says "Python 3.x developer", your resume should say "Python" not just "programming". Copy key phrases directly.

### 3. Use standard section headings

"Work Experience" beats "Professional Journey". "Skills" beats "What I Bring". ATSs look for predictable labels.

### 4. Include both Hebrew and English keywords

Many Israeli job postings list requirements in both languages. Mirror them in the same language as the posting.

### 5. Quantify your achievements

"Reduced deployment time by 40%" beats "improved deployment process". Numbers help both ATSs and human readers.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] Single-column layout
- [ ] Standard section headings in the posting's language
- [ ] Key skills from the JD appear verbatim in your resume
- [ ] Dates in consistent format (MM/YYYY or YYYY)
- [ ] No images, logos, or text boxes
- [ ] Exported as PDF, not Word (unless specifically requested)
